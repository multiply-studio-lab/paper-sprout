Korean Numbers for Beginners: Sino-Korean vs Native Numbers

Korean has two complete sets of numbers. You say 세 시 for three o'clock but 삼 분 for three minutes. It looks confusing at first, but a few simple rules tell you which set to use almost every time.

Paper Sprout Korean · November 2026

The two systems side by side

Sino-Korean numbers (일, 이, 삼) came from Chinese characters. Native Korean numbers (하나, 둘, 셋) are older Korean words. In front of a counter, the native numbers 1–4 and 20 get shorter: 하나 → 한, 둘 → 두, 셋 → 세, 넷 → 네, 스물 → 스무.

The tens

Sino-Korean tens are regular (two-ten, three-ten). Native tens each have their own word, so they are worth memorizing.

Native numbers stop at 99. For 100 and above, Koreans use Sino-Korean numbers, even when counting things: 백 개 (100 things).

Big numbers: think in 만 (10,000)

English groups digits in thousands. Korean groups them in ten-thousands: 만 is 10,000 and 억 is 100,000,000. So 230,000 is "23 만", 이십삼만. When you write numbers in Hangul, put a space after each 만 group. Also, drop 일 before 십, 백, 천, 만 at the start of a number: 10 is 십, 1,000 is 천, 10,000 is 만.

Which system when?

Use Sino-Korean for…Use native Korean for…
minutes and seconds: 삼십 분hours on the clock: 세 시
money: 오천 원counting things: 사과 두 개
dates: 유월 십오 일counting people: 학생 세 명
floors, rooms, bus numbers: 삼 층, 백일 호age in everyday speech: 스무 살
months of time: 삼 개월hours of time: 두 시간
phone numbers, math, scoreshow many times: 세 번
Two months change their sound: June is 유월 (not 육월) and October is 시월 (not 십월).

Telling time: both systems at once

The hour uses native numbers with 시, and the minutes use Sino-Korean numbers with 분. 3:30 is 세 시 삼십 분, or simply 세 시 반 (half past three). 7:45 is 일곱 시 사십오 분.

To ask a price, say 얼마예요? (How much is it?). To ask how many, put 몇 in front of the counter: 몇 개예요?, 몇 시예요?, 몇 살이에요?

Quick self-check

  1. Two cups of coffee: 커피 ___ 잔
  2. 9:20: ___ 시 ___ 분
  3. 20 years old: ___ 살
  4. June 6: ___ 육 일

Answers: 두 · 아홉, 이십 · 스무 · 유월
