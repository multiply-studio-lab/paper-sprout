Korean Verb Conjugation for Beginners: Present, Past and Future

Korean verbs change their endings all the time: 가다 becomes 가요, 갔어요, 갈 거예요. The good news is that one small rule does most of the work. Here it is, step by step, in the polite form you will use every day.

Paper Sprout Korean · November 2026

Step 1: find the stem

Every Korean verb and adjective in the dictionary ends in 다. Take 다 off and what is left is the stem. All endings attach to the stem.

Dictionary formStemMeaning
가다가to go
먹다먹to eat
공부하다공부하to study
좋다좋to be good

In Korean, adjectives like 좋다 (to be good) conjugate exactly like verbs, so everything below works for both.

Step 2: the present tense, -아요 or -어요

For the polite present tense, look at the last vowel of the stem:

  • If it is ㅏ or ㅗ, add -아요.
  • If it is anything else, add -어요.
  • If the stem ends in 하, it becomes 해요.
VerbLast vowelPresent (polite)
살다 (to live)ㅏ살아요
좋다 (to be good)ㅗ좋아요
앉다 (to sit)ㅏ앉아요
먹다 (to eat)ㅓ먹어요
읽다 (to read)ㅣ읽어요
있다 (to have, to be there)ㅣ있어요
공부하다 (to study)하공부해요

Step 3: when the stem ends in a vowel, the vowels merge

If the stem has no final consonant, the two vowels usually squeeze into one syllable. This is why you hear 가요, not 가아요.

VerbWhat happensPresent
가다 (to go)가 + 아요 → 가요가요
서다 (to stand)서 + 어요 → 서요서요
오다 (to come)ㅗ + 아 → ㅘ와요
보다 (to see)ㅗ + 아 → ㅘ봐요
배우다 (to learn)ㅜ + 어 → ㅝ배워요
마시다 (to drink)ㅣ + 어 → ㅕ마셔요
되다 (to become)ㅚ + 어 → ㅙ돼요
The ㅡ drop. A stem ending in ㅡ loses the ㅡ: 쓰다 (to write) → 써요, 크다 (to be big) → 커요. If the vowel before it is ㅏ or ㅗ, you use ㅏ: 바쁘다 (to be busy) → 바빠요.

Step 4: the past tense is the present plus ㅆ

Once you can make the present, the past is easy. Take the present form, drop 요, add ㅆ under the last syllable, and finish with 어요.

PresentPastMeaning
가요갔어요went
먹어요먹었어요ate
봐요봤어요saw
마셔요마셨어요drank
공부해요공부했어요studied

Step 5: the future, -(으)ㄹ 거예요

For plans and predictions, add -ㄹ 거예요 to a stem with no final consonant, and -을 거예요 to a stem that ends in a consonant. Stems ending in ㄹ just add 거예요.

VerbFutureMeaning
가다갈 거예요will go
마시다마실 거예요will drink
먹다먹을 거예요will eat
살다살 거예요will live
공부하다공부할 거예요will study

Bonus: saying “not”

Put 안 before the verb, or add -지 않아요 to the stem. Both are correct; 안 is more common in speech.

  • 안 가요 = 가지 않아요 (I don’t go)
  • 안 먹어요 = 먹지 않아요 (I don’t eat)
  • With 하다 verbs, 안 goes right before 해요: 공부 안 해요 (I don’t study).

A word about irregular verbs

A few groups of common verbs change their stem before -아요/-어요. You don’t need to master them today, but it helps to recognise them:

GroupExamplePresent
ㅂ irregular덥다 (to be hot)더워요
ㄷ irregular듣다 (to listen)들어요
르 irregular모르다 (to not know)몰라요
ㅅ irregular낫다 (to get better)나아요

Not every verb that looks like these is irregular: 입다 (to wear) is regular, 입어요, and 받다 (to receive) is regular, 받아요. Learn the irregular ones as you meet them.

The short version

  1. Take 다 off to get the stem.
  2. Last vowel ㅏ/ㅗ → -아요; anything else → -어요; 하 → 해요.
  3. Past: present minus 요, plus ㅆ어요. Future: -(으)ㄹ 거예요.
