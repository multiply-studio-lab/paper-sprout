Korean Batchim Made Simple: The 7 Final Sounds

Korean has lots of letters that can sit at the bottom of a syllable, but at the end of a syllable they are pronounced as only seven sounds. Learn the seven, and batchim stops being scary.

Paper Sprout Korean · November 2026

Once you can read simple syllables like 가 and 나, you meet syllables with a consonant at the bottom: 각, 난, 옷, 꽃, 앞. That bottom consonant is called batchim (받침), which literally means “support” or “prop”, because it sits under the syllable like a base.

Batchim confuses beginners for one simple reason: the letter you see is not always the sound you say. 옷 (clothes) is spelled with ㅅ, but it sounds like 옫. 앞 (front) is spelled with ㅍ, but it sounds like 압. The rule behind this is short and very regular.

The rule: only 7 sounds at the end of a syllable

At the end of a syllable, Korean only allows seven consonant sounds. Every final consonant, whatever letter is written, is pronounced as one of these seven:

A handy way to remember the seven: ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅇ are simply the first seven consonants of the alphabet with ㅅ skipped. If you know the alphabet order (ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅅ ㅇ), drop ㅅ and you have them.

Why it happens: the sound is “held”, not released

In English, when you say “cat” you can let a little puff of air out at the end. In Korean, final ㄱ, ㄷ and ㅂ are unreleased: your mouth moves into position and stops, with no puff of air afterward. Try this:

  • Say 밥 (bap) and keep your lips closed at the end. Don’t open them.
  • Say 책 (chaek) and stop with the back of your tongue pressed up, without letting air out.
  • Say 옷 (ot) and stop with the tip of your tongue behind your top teeth.

Once a sound is held like that, there is no way to hear the difference between ㅅ, ㅈ, ㅊ or ㅌ. They all become the same tongue-tip stop, which Korean writes as ㄷ. The same logic merges ㅍ into ㅂ, and ㅋ and ㄲ into ㄱ.

This is also why romanization writes these endings with k, t, p: 책 is chaek, 옷 is ot, 밥 is bap, even though the letters are the same ones that sound like g, d, b at the start of a word.

The big exception: when a vowel comes next

The seven-sound rule applies when the batchim is at the end: at the end of a word, or before another consonant. When the next syllable is a particle or ending that starts with a vowel (that is, with a silent ㅇ), the batchim moves over and is pronounced as the first sound of that next syllable, with its full original sound. This is called linking (연음).

(Compound words follow a slightly different rule: 맛없다, “to taste bad”, is pronounced [마덥따]. You can safely leave that for later. 맛있다 is a well-known exception: both [마딛따] and [마싣따] are standard, and 맛있어요 is usually said [마시써요].)

WrittenSaidMeaning
옷이[오시]clothes (subject)
꽃을[꼬츨]flower (object)
앞에[아페]in front
부엌에서[부어케서]in the kitchen
밖에[바께]outside
있어요[이써요]there is / I have

So the spelling is not random. Korean keeps the “real” consonant in writing because you hear it as soon as a vowel follows. 옷 sounds like 옫 alone, but 옷이 clearly has an s sound: [오시].

One special case: ㅇ as a batchim never moves, because it stands for the “ng” sound. The ㅇ in 강에 (in the river) stays where it is: [강에]. And a final ㅎ usually goes silent before a vowel: 좋아요 is pronounced [조아요]. Before ㄱ, ㄷ or ㅈ it makes them aspirated instead: 좋다 is [조타].

Double batchim: pick one of the two

Some syllables have two different consonants at the bottom, like 닭 (chicken) or 값 (price). At the end of a word, or before a consonant, only one of the two is pronounced, and it is then pronounced as one of the seven sounds:

WordSaidMeaning
닭[닥]chicken
값[갑]price
여덟[여덜]eight
삶[삼]life
앉다[안따]to sit
읽다[익따]to read

Which one survives depends on the combination, and there are a few exceptions (for example 밟다, “to step on”, is [밥따]). As a beginner, simply learn these words one by one as you meet them. When a vowel follows, both consonants are heard: 닭이 is [달기], 값이 is [갑씨].

You may notice that 가락 in 숟가락 (spoon) sounds like [까락]. After the stops ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ, the next plain consonant usually becomes tense. That is the next sound rule to learn, and you will pick it up naturally by listening.

Three common beginner mistakes

  • Adding a vowel at the end. English speakers often say 밥 as “ba-puh” or 책 as “chae-kuh”. Stop the sound cleanly instead: close your lips for 밥, and just stop for 책.
  • Pronouncing the spelling. Saying 옷 with a hissing “s” at the end sounds foreign to Korean ears. Alone, it is [옫]. Save the s sound for 옷이 [오시].
  • Mixing up ㄴ and ㅇ at the end. 반 (half, class) and 방 (room) are different words. For ㄴ, the tip of your tongue touches behind your top teeth; for ㅇ, the back of your tongue rises and the tip stays down, as in English “sing”.

The short version

  1. At the end of a syllable there are only seven sounds: ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅇ.
  2. ㅋ ㄲ → ㄱ; ㅍ → ㅂ; ㅅ ㅆ ㅈ ㅊ ㅌ ㅎ → ㄷ.
  3. If a vowel comes next, the batchim slides over and keeps its real sound.

That is genuinely most of it. Write a few of these words by hand while saying them aloud and the pattern will start to feel natural.
