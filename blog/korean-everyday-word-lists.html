Korean Everyday Word Lists: Food, Family, Days of the Week and Question Words

Four small themed lists you will use constantly, each with romanization and a short example sentence to read aloud and copy by hand.

Paper Sprout Korean · November 2026

Big vocabulary lists are hard to finish. Small themed lists are not. Here are four that come up in almost every beginner conversation: food, family, the days of the week, and question words. Each word has its Revised Romanization underneath and, where it helps, an example sentence in polite everyday Korean (the style ending in -요).

10 Korean food words

These are dishes and ingredients you will see on almost any Korean menu or in any Korean kitchen.

I eat fruit often.

And the phrase to go with them: 맛있어요! (masisseoyo), “It’s delicious!” Notice that the final ㅅ of 맛 moves into the next syllable when you say it: [마시써요]. Our batchim guide explains why.

Note: 밥 means cooked rice, but it is also the everyday word for “a meal”. Uncooked rice is a different word, 쌀 (ssal).

Korean family words

Korean family words are more specific than English ones. The most famous example is “older brother” and “older sister”, which depend on who is speaking:

said by a boy or mansaid by a girl or woman
older brother형
hyeong		오빠
oppa
older sister누나
nuna		언니
eonni

So a woman talking about her older brother says 오빠, and a man talking about his older brother says 형. These words are also used for close older friends, which is why you hear 오빠 and 언니 so often in K-dramas. Younger siblings are simply 동생, whoever is speaking.

KoreanMeaningExample
아버지
abeoji		father아버지는 회사원이에요.
My father is an office worker.
어머니
eomeoni		mother어머니가 요리를 하세요.
My mother is cooking.
할아버지
harabeoji		grandfather할아버지는 건강하세요.
My grandfather is healthy.
할머니
halmeoni		grandmother할머니 댁에 가요.
I’m going to my grandmother’s house.
형
hyeong		older brother (said by a male)형은 키가 커요.
My older brother is tall.
오빠
oppa		older brother (said by a female)오빠는 대학생이에요.
My older brother is a college student.
누나
nuna		older sister (said by a male)누나가 선물을 줬어요.
My older sister gave me a gift.
언니
eonni		older sister (said by a female)언니와 영화를 봤어요.
I watched a movie with my older sister.
동생
dongsaeng		younger sibling동생이 한 명 있어요.
I have one younger sibling.
가족
gajok		family우리 가족은 네 명이에요.
There are four people in my family.

In casual speech, children and adults alike say 엄마 (eomma, mom) and 아빠 (appa, dad). 아버지 and 어머니 are the more formal words. Also notice 우리 가족, literally “our family”: Korean usually says “our” where English says “my” for family and home.

Days of the week

Every day ends in 요일 (yoil), “day of the week”. The first syllable is what changes, and there is a nice memory trick: the days are named after the sun, the moon, and five elements.

KoreanDayFirst syllable means
월요일
woryoil		Monday월 moon
화요일
hwayoil		Tuesday화 fire
수요일
suyoil		Wednesday수 water
목요일
mogyoil		Thursday목 tree, wood
금요일
geumyoil		Friday금 gold, metal
토요일
toyoil		Saturday토 earth, soil
일요일
iryoil		Sunday일 sun

The romanization looks odd for some days because it follows pronunciation: in 월요일 the ㄹ slides into the next syllable, so it sounds like [워료일], woryoil.

The question and answer to practice:

오늘은 무슨 요일이에요?
What day (of the week) is it today?

오늘은 월요일이에요.
Today is Monday.

Swap in any day to answer. The weekend is 주말 (jumal): 주말에 뭐 해요? “What do you do on the weekend?”

8 question words

With these eight words you can ask almost anything. In Korean, the question word usually sits right where the answer would go, and the sentence still ends with the verb.

KoreanMeaningExample
누구
nugu		who이 사람은 누구예요?
Who is this person?
언제
eonje		when생일이 언제예요?
When is your birthday?
어디
eodi		where집이 어디예요?
Where is your house?
뭐
mwo		what이름이 뭐예요?
What is your name?
왜
wae		why왜 늦었어요?
Why were you late?
어떻게
eotteoke		how학교에 어떻게 가요?
How do you get to school?
얼마
eolma		how much이거 얼마예요?
How much is this?
몇
myeot		how many가족이 몇 명이에요?
How many people are in your family?

Two notes. 뭐 is the short, spoken form of 무엇; you will hear 뭐 far more often. And 몇 is always followed by a counting word, like 명 for people in 몇 명, “how many people”. 얼마 is mostly used for prices: 이거 얼마예요? is the question you will use in every shop.

How to practice these lists

  1. Pick one list per day. Read every word aloud twice.
  2. Write each Korean word once by hand, saying it as you write.
  3. Cover the Korean column and try to write it again from the English.
  4. Copy two example sentences, then change one word in each: 오늘은 월요일이에요 → 오늘은 금요일이에요.
